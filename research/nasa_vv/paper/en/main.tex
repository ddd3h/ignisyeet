\begin{abstract}
IgnisYeet is a rocket simulator whose aerodynamic tool drives the open-source flow solver SU2.
We apply the verification and validation procedure of the NASA WIND/NPARC tutorial (AIAA G-077-1998) to it in three layers:
the solver alone against exact solutions (layer~0), the same cone through the product's whole chain from CAD geometry to force coefficients (layer~1), and the tutorial's six steps on a sample rocket and on the Basic Finner, including comparison with free-flight measurements (layer~2).
On four exact solutions, SU2 run with the numerical settings the product writes reproduces the exact value to within 0.2\,\% on the surface pressure and the shock angle in the primary setups, but wall quantities derived from the Mach number retain errors of 0.8 to 3.6\,\% that decrease slowly or not at all.
The grid convergence index (GCI) bounds the true discretisation error for 5 of 7 inviscid results and for 2 of 5 laminar plate results, the latter only where the error changes sign; neither the observed order nor its stability across windows detects both failures, so a GCI computed without an exact answer can understate the true error even when its order looks plausible.
Grids generated by the product cannot be refined similarly, because the mesher fixes the largest cell size; a four-level Basic Finner study at Mach 2.375 does not establish a GCI.
The NPARC reference table for a 10$^\circ$ cone is internally inconsistent: its shock angle disagrees with independent tables, its pressure, temperature and density ratios disagree with the Taylor--Maccoll solution computed here, and only its surface Mach number agrees.
Through the product's chain the same cone could not be brought to an accepted finest level, so layer~1 has no GCI and no pipeline error, including three-dimensional unstructured meshing, is quoted.
On the sample rocket the only GCI that is neither refused nor unusable is that of the Mach 2 normal-force slope, 10.6\,\% on one definition of grid size and 16.3\,\% on the other (flagged as outside the asymptotic range); the Mach 2 centre-of-pressure GCI is flagged as unreliable and is not used.
For the Basic Finner with RANS at Mach 1.832 no GCI was obtained, so the validation comparison has no RANS band and whether the numerical and measurement bands overlap cannot be judged.
None of the seven Euler-against-RANS conditions can be given a verdict.
On a turbulent flat plate run in SU2 directly, the Menter SST model differs from Spalart--Allmaras by $-0.79\,\%$ in skin friction, within the SST GCI of 1.14\,\% (the Spalart--Allmaras GCI is flagged), and by $-0.46\,\%$ in drag, which is not separable from the grid uncertainty.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Aerodynamic coefficients from a flow solver are numbers without a stated reliability unless they carry an uncertainty.
The verification and validation procedure of the NASA Glenn WIND/NPARC project \citep{nparc_tutorial}, which follows the AIAA guide \citep{aiaa1998}, divides the task into six steps: examination of the code, iterative convergence, consistency, spatial (grid) convergence, temporal convergence, and comparison with a more accurate solution or with measurements.
Its central quantity is the grid convergence index of \citet{roache1998} in the form given by \citet{celik2008}.
A comparison without an uncertainty band cannot say which side is wrong.

IgnisYeet is a rocket simulator.
Its aerodynamic tool takes a CAD description of the rocket, builds an unstructured volume mesh, runs SU2 \citep{economon2016} and returns force and moment coefficients to the flight simulator.
This paper applies the tutorial to that tool.
The deliverable is a numerical uncertainty band on the coefficients the tool returns, together with a statement of where such a band can and cannot be computed.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
\emph{Verification} asks whether the equations are solved correctly and compares a result with an exact or otherwise highly accurate solution.
\emph{Cross-comparison} compares one method or program with another; it shows that they differ or agree, not which is closer to reality.
\emph{Validation} asks whether the model resembles the real system and compares a result with measured data.
Only the comparison with free-flight measurements (Section~\ref{sec:validation}) is validation; layers~0 and~1 are verification.

\subsection{Three layers}
\label{sec:layers}

% source: results/layer0.csv (header: SU2 version)
The study has three layers (Table~\ref{tab:layers}).
Layers~0 and~1 solve the same exact problem, a cone of 10$^\circ$ half angle in supersonic flow, by different routes: layer~0 runs SU2~8.3.0 directly, layer~1 runs the product's whole chain.
Both are compared with the same Taylor--Maccoll solution, and the difference of their errors is the part due to the chain around the solver.
The product accepts only a rocket geometry, so layer~0 does not exercise its pipeline and no statement that the pipeline was verified rests on layer~0.
Layer~0 uses two-dimensional and axisymmetric structured grids; layers~1 and~2 use three-dimensional unstructured half models.
The difference between layers~0 and~1 thus includes the effect of three-dimensional unstructured meshing and is reported as \emph{pipeline error, including three-dimensional unstructured meshing}, not as meshing error alone.

\subsection{Grid convergence index and the gate}
\label{sec:gci}

For three solutions $f_1,f_2,f_3$ on grids of representative size $h_1<h_2<h_3$, finest first, with refinement ratios $r_{21}=h_2/h_1$ and $r_{32}=h_3/h_2$, let $\varepsilon_{21}=f_2-f_1$ and $\varepsilon_{32}=f_3-f_2$.
For a constant ratio $r$ the tutorial gives the observed order $p=\ln\!\big[(f_3-f_2)/(f_2-f_1)\big]/\ln r$, the extrapolated value $f_{h0}=f_1+(f_1-f_2)/(r^p-1)$ and $\mathrm{GCI}=F_s\,|\varepsilon|/(r^p-1)$.
Where the ratio varies we use the procedure of \citet{celik2008}: with $s=\operatorname{sign}(\varepsilon_{32}/\varepsilon_{21})$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
p&=\frac{1}{\ln r_{21}}\Big|\ln\Big|\frac{\varepsilon_{32}}{\varepsilon_{21}}\Big|+q(p)\Big|,\\
q(p)&=\ln\frac{r_{21}^{p}-s}{r_{32}^{p}-s},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
solved by fixed-point iteration, then $f_\mathrm{ext}=(r_{21}^{p}f_1-f_2)/(r_{21}^{p}-1)$ and $\mathrm{GCI}_\fine=F_s\,e_a/(r_{21}^{p}-1)$ with $e_a=|(f_1-f_2)/f_1|$.
The factor of safety is $F_s=1.25$ for three grids and $3$ for two.
The asymptotic-range check is $r_{21}^{p}\,\mathrm{GCI}_{21}/\mathrm{GCI}_{32}$, near one in the asymptotic range.
The grid size is $h=N^{-1/3}$ from the node count $N$ for three-dimensional grids (the Basic Finner Euler family records cells only, so $N$ is the cell count there) and $h=N_\mathrm{cells}^{-1/2}$ for the two-dimensional layer~0 grids, which are refined exactly by a factor of two in each direction.

% source: results/gci_existing.csv, results/layer0_gci.csv (headers: the gate, the order flags and the oscillatory rule)
Three rules decide when a number is reported.
\begin{itemize}
\item A grid level enters a study if the solver reported convergence or, where it gave no verdict, the residual fell by at least three orders of magnitude; a level the solver reported as not converged, or one with neither piece of evidence, is refused, and the refusal states the reason.
\item A computed order above 4 (twice the formal second order) or below 0.5 (a quarter of it) is flagged: the triplet is then outside the asymptotic range and its GCI is not reliable.
\item A triplet whose differences change sign (oscillatory convergence, $\varepsilon_{32}/\varepsilon_{21}<0$) is labelled and receives \emph{no} uncertainty band.
  \citet{celik2008} do compute a value for such a case; this study does not, and reports none.
\end{itemize}
Where the refinement is not geometrically similar, so that no single definition of $h$ is justified, the GCI is computed under every defensible definition and both results are shown (Section~\ref{sec:grid}).

\paragraph{Acceptance of the procedure.}
% source: tests/test_gci.py, tests/data/prD.out (acceptance tests of the GCI procedure; not a results CSV)
The implementation reproduces the worked example of the tutorial \citep{nparc_spatial} (three grids, $r=2$): it matches the printed output of the tutorial's own program, namely the order, the extrapolated value, both GCI values and the asymptotic ratio, to a relative $10^{-4}$.
That program is single precision, so its fifth digit differs from a double-precision evaluation; the double-precision values that the tests also pin are the study's own numbers, a regression check and not an independent published reference.
Because that example has $r_{21}=r_{32}$, the variable-ratio path is also tested against two monotone columns of Table~1 of \citet{celik2008}, which have unequal ratios, to the precision that table prints.
One cell of that table, the extrapolated relative error in the second column, is printed as a value that differs from the value recomputed from the same table's own printed entries; the cell is not used in the test.
The third column of that table is oscillatory; the implementation refuses it by the rule above rather than reproduce the table's value.

\subsection{Exact solutions and layer 0 setup}
\label{sec:exact}
\label{sec:l0setup}

The exact solutions are computed, not transcribed: the weak-solution oblique shock relation, the Taylor--Maccoll cone equation integrated from the shock to the surface with the shock angle found by shooting, the Prandtl--Meyer function, and the Blasius similarity solution \citep{blasius1908,schlichting2017}, all for a perfect gas with $\gamma=1.4$.
The oblique-shock and Prandtl--Meyer results agree with the tabulated values of the NPARC archive pages \citep{nparc_wedge,nparc_pm15} within the precision of those tables.
The cone solution is checked against independent tables in Section~\ref{sec:cone10}.
The Taylor--Maccoll solver in the product's documentation brackets the shock angle by a fixed upper limit that, for large cone angles near detachment, lies on the wrong side of the maximum of the cone-angle curve; the 10$^\circ$ cones used here are far from that range.
% source: no results CSV (comparison of the two solvers; not used for any number in this paper)
% source: results/layer0.csv (header: exact references and conditions)
The cases follow the NPARC archive \citep{nparc_archive}: a 15$^\circ$ wedge and a 15$^\circ$ expansion at Mach 2.5, a 10$^\circ$ half-angle cone at Mach 2.35, and a laminar flat plate.
The plate is run at Mach 0.2.
The Blasius solution is incompressible, so a compressibility correction is estimated and reported alongside.

% source: results/layer0.csv (header), results/layer0_gci.csv (header), results/layer0_plate.csv (header)
The numerical settings of the layer~0 Euler runs (convective discretisation, reconstruction, limiter, gradient method, CFL control, linear solver and stopping criteria) are copied from a configuration that the product itself wrote for an Euler case; a test asserts that they are identical to the product's.
The only permitted differences are the boundary markers and, for the cone, the axisymmetric switch.
The gas model is the product's.
The product has no laminar mode, so the laminar plate is run with the Navier--Stokes equations and without a turbulence model, using the flow numerics and gas model of a product-written RANS configuration.
It therefore checks the product's RANS discretisation with the turbulence model removed; it is not a configuration the product itself writes.
The product starts a RANS case with a first-order stage and restarts at second order; the layer~0 plate is started cold at second order.
That the steady solution does not depend on this path was not tested.

The grids are body-fitted and structured, with three levels of $65\times49$, $129\times97$ and $257\times193$ nodes ($85\times49$, $169\times97$, $337\times193$ for the plate), exactly nested so that $r=2$ holds exactly.
In the supersonic cases the inlet, outlet and top are far-field boundaries, and every wave leaves through the outlet: along the top boundary $|p/\pinf-1|\le7.3\times10^{-10}$ in each of the twelve runs.
The runs are driven to a density residual of $10^{-14}$ or to an iteration budget.
The convergence measure reported below is the peak minus the final value of $\log_{10}$ of the density residual.

\subsection{Layers 1 and 2}
\label{sec:l12setup}

% source: results/layer1_cone.csv (header and level columns)
Layer~1 solves the layer~0 cone, Mach 2.35, zero incidence, Euler, as a 10$^\circ$ cone-cylinder through the product on a three-dimensional unstructured half model.
The body has a base radius of 0.05\,m, a cone length of 0.2836\,m, a cylinder to 0.5836\,m, and the product's Euler tail fairing behind it; layer~0 has a bare cone in a rectangular domain and no cylinder or tail.
Three levels have wall cell sizes of 3.92, 2.80 and 2.00\,mm (a factor 1.4); the body has no fins, so the wall size is the only refinement setting.
They have 28\,887, 45\,258 and 75\,796 nodes (132\,328, 209\,052 and 350\,868 cells), a growth of 1.57 and 1.67 in nodes against $1.4^3=2.74$ for a similar refinement, so these grids are not similar either.
The product refuses a single incidence, so each level also solves $4^\circ$; only the zero-incidence case is used, and convergence is read from it alone.
All runs used one build of the product (the study's base commit) on one node with 8, 10 and 14 ranks for the three levels; the rank count also sets the domain decomposition, and whether the fine level's behaviour depends on it was not tested.
The surface pressure coefficient $\Cp=(p-\pinf)/(\tfrac12\rho_\infty V_\infty^2)$ of the wall nodes lying within 1\,\% of the cone radius is binned along $x/L_\mathrm{cone}$ in bins of 0.1 from 0.2 to 0.9 and compared with the Taylor--Maccoll value 0.09673.
The window $0.4\le x/L_\mathrm{cone}\le0.9$ is the like-for-like counterpart of layer~0's wall window; the other summaries (the 0.2--0.9 mean and the nearest-tip bin) have none.
The freestream state of layer~1 is the product's launch altitude (20\,m, $p=101\,085$\,Pa, $T=288.02$\,K), that of layer~0 $p=101\,325$\,Pa, $T=288.15$\,K; Mach number and $\gamma$ are the same.

% source: results/gci_existing.csv (Basic Finner Euler conditions and levels), results/consistency.csv (sample-rocket Mach numbers)
Layer~2 treats a sample rocket (Euler, Mach 0.3, 0.8 and 2.0, incidence 0$^\circ$ and 4$^\circ$) and the Basic Finner, a cone-cylinder body with four rectangular fins.
The Basic Finner is run with Euler at Mach 2.375 (incidence 0$^\circ$, 2$^\circ$, 4$^\circ$; four existing levels) and with RANS (Spalart--Allmaras) at Mach 1.832 (incidence 0$^\circ$ and 2$^\circ$).
Mach 1.832 is chosen for RANS because it is the one point at which the product's RANS converges and at which free-flight data exist; at Mach 2.375 the product's RANS stalls in a limit cycle and at Mach 3.147 it diverges.
% source: doc/source/validation.rst (RANS at Mach 3.147: alpha = 0 diverged twice, alpha = 2 deg not converged; not a results CSV)
The normal-force slope $\CNa$ is $C_N(2^\circ)/2^\circ$ for the Basic Finner, following the existing convention.
% source: results/sample_euler_levels.csv, results/gci_sample_euler.csv (headers)
The sample-rocket Euler family has wall and fin sizes of 3.92/1.96, 2.80/1.40 and 2.00/1.00\,mm, with 98\,151, 170\,830 and 302\,918 nodes (458\,640, 798\,534 and 1\,416\,454 cells): a growth of 1.74 and 1.77 in cells against $1.4^3=2.74$ for a similar refinement.
The density residual fell by 3.2 to 10.9 orders at zero incidence and 6.6 to 8.7 at $4^\circ$.
The mesh is mirrored about the symmetry plane, so $-4^\circ$ is the exact mirror of $+4^\circ$ and is not solved; $\CNa=C_N(4^\circ)/4^\circ$ is formed from the $+4^\circ$ solve, and $x_{cp}$ is that of the combined row.
A medium level meshed without the mirror, with both $+4^\circ$ and $-4^\circ$ solved, serves the antisymmetry check only and is not a member of the family.
% source: results/basic_finner_rans_levels.csv (header and level columns)
The Basic Finner RANS family has wall sizes of 2.1, 1.5 and 1.07\,mm and a fin size equal to the wall size, with 328\,364, 533\,985 and 926\,390 nodes (813\,498, 1\,265\,515 and 2\,145\,093 cells): a cell growth of 1.56 and 1.70 against 2.74.
Incidence $0^\circ$ and $2^\circ$ are used ($-2^\circ$ is also solved by the mirror setting and not used), and $\CNa=C_N(2^\circ)/2^\circ$.
A fin size smaller than the wall size, as originally planned (about 0.22 of it), cannot be used: with RANS and the CAD surface, the product stops with a meshing error (a memory-corruption abort of the mesher) on every build tried (the coarse and medium levels; the fine level was not tried); whether the product's input or gmsh is at fault was not separated, and the product was not modified.
The medium level has the wall and fin sizes of the existing RANS Basic Finner run, but a different mesh (5.3\,\% fewer nodes, 3.4\,\% fewer cells), so it is the nearest level to that run and not that run.

\paragraph{Grids built by the product cannot be similar.}
% source: results/gci_existing.csv (header and n1..n3 columns of basic_finner_mesh_check)
Refining the product's two cell-size settings together refines only the near-wall region.
The mesher grades the volume mesh from the wall cell size up to a largest cell size that is one tenth of the far-field radius; that largest size is not a user setting and does not scale with the wall size, and scaling it would require changing the far-field radius, which changes the problem solved.
For the four existing Basic Finner Euler levels, where wall and fin sizes scale by 1.4 per level, the cell count grows by 1.70, 1.77 and 1.83 per level against $1.4^3=2.74$ for a similar refinement.
Neither $h\propto N^{-1/3}$ nor $h\propto$ wall size is then the effective grid size.
We compute every GCI of such a family under both definitions and treat the spread between them as part of the uncertainty; the two definitions bracket the question and the spread is not a bound on the true GCI.

\subsection{A reference table that disagrees with itself}

Two features of the table need to be read with care.
First, the observed orders range from 0.39 to 3.39, against a formal second-order scheme, and are not a measurement of the scheme's order.
The wall windows contain the tip or corner transient, and the shock angle is read from a captured, smeared shock; the notes of the result file describe the low orders as consistent with these causes rather than with a smooth-field discretisation error.
For the wedge wall pressure the order is stable across four moving windows (0.84 to 1.04); for the expansion wall pressure it is not (0.65 to 2.54).
Second, the wall Mach number on the expansion does not converge: the three grids give 3.1214, 3.1216 and 3.1218 against an exact 3.2368, an error of $-3.57$, $-3.56$ and $-3.55\,\%$, essentially unchanged by refinement, so no order or GCI exists although the run itself converged (13.1 orders on the finest grid).
A GCI is a statement about discretisation error that shrinks with the grid, and this error does not.

\paragraph{Wall entropy.}
% source: results/layer0.csv (diag_entropy_* rows and header)
Along the wall the entropy exceeds the exact value on all three cases, while ten rows off the wall it is at the exact value and upstream of the corner or tip it is exact.
On the expansion the wall entropy ratio $s/s_\infty$ is 1.0785, 1.0784 and 1.0783 on the three grids, a constant offset of about 7.8\,\%; on the wedge the wall reads 1.058 on all three grids against the exact post-shock value 1.030 (also found ten rows off the wall on the two finer grids), a grid-independent excess of 2.7\,\%; on the cone the excess falls with the grid (3.76, 1.98 and 1.06\,\% in the surface window).
This is consistent with entropy generated at the corner, leading edge or tip and convected along the wall streamline; that is an interpretation, not a proof.
The surface pressure on the expansion has an error of 0.0097\,\% while the wall Mach number has $-3.55\,\%$, so quantities obtained from the wall pressure show no comparable error, whereas quantities taken from the wall Mach number, density or temperature would be expected to carry the excess.

\paragraph{The tip junction.}
% source: results/layer0.csv (cone and cone_sym_axis rows), results/layer0_gci.csv (header)
The cone is run twice.
In the first run the symmetry axis and the cone surface form one slip-wall boundary; in the second the axis is a symmetry boundary and the cone surface a slip wall.
In the second run the node at the tip belongs to both boundaries and is a stagnation node on every grid (Mach 0, $p=2.76\,\pinf$), whereas in the first run it is regular (Mach 2.23, $p=1.17\,\pinf$).
The finest-grid error of the wall pressure coefficient is $-0.19\,\%$ in the first run and $-0.75\,\%$ in the second, and of the wall Mach number $-0.80$ and $-2.98\,\%$: most of the second run's error is the boundary-condition junction.
The first run is taken as the layer~0 cone result.
On the expansion, splitting the lower boundary into two slip-wall boundaries at the corner made the corner node a stagnation point and stalled the density residual near $-1.5$ over 20\,000 iterations.
These are sensitivities of a two-dimensional structured setup.
The product's three-dimensional meshes also have boundary junctions (wall to base), but whether they are affected in the same way was not tested.

\subsection{Does the GCI bound the true error?}
\label{sec:gcibound}

% source: results/layer0_gci.csv (true_error_pct, gci_bounds_error; header)
The purpose of a GCI is to bound the discretisation error.
Layer~0 is the only place in this study where the exact answer is known, so it can test whether it does.
Of the seven inviscid rows with a computed GCI, the bound held for five and failed for two: the cone wall pressure coefficient (GCI 0.059\,\% against a finest-grid error of 0.188\,\%, so the true error is 3.2 times the GCI) and the same quantity in the symmetry-axis variant (0.71\,\% against 0.75\,\%).
One further row, the expansion wall Mach number, has no GCI.

``Held'' does not mean tight.
The ratio of GCI to true error is about 8 for the expansion wall pressure, 3.1 for the cone wall Mach number and 1.5 for the symmetry-axis cone wall Mach number (ratios of the entries of Table~\ref{tab:l0}).
A bound that is eight times the error is a bound, not an estimate.
Rows flagged as outside the asymptotic range (orders below 0.5 or above 4) are included in the count of five.

The practical question is whether a failure can be detected when no exact answer exists, which is the situation in layer~2 (Table~\ref{tab:detect}).
The order flag (outside 0.5 to 4) misses both failures, because the cone wall pressure coefficient has $p=3.39$.
Stability of the order across four moving windows (within 0.5) catches the cone failure, whose orders range from 2.10 to 4.12, but misses the symmetry-axis failure, whose orders are stable at 1.36 to 1.58; and it flags the expansion wall pressure as unstable (0.65 to 2.54) although the GCI bounded that error.
Neither available check is therefore sufficient, and a GCI computed without an exact answer can understate the true error even when its order looks plausible and its windows look stable.

What remains after extrapolation is $+0.97\,\%$ on $C_f$, which the GCI cannot account for.

The difference from Blasius mixes several things that a discretisation-error bound does not cover.
(i)~The outer flow at $\eta=8$ is $1.0036\,U$ on every grid, and for a boundary layer under an edge velocity $u_e$ the skin friction scales as $(u_e/U)^{3/2}$; $C_f/(u_e/U)^{3/2}$ on the fine grid is $0.53\,\%$ below Blasius (an estimate that takes $u_e$ at one height).
(ii)~An Eckert reference-temperature estimate of compressibility at Mach 0.2 changes $C_f$ by $-0.054\,\%$ (an estimate; the CFD value is not corrected).
(iii)~The domain matters.
In a first, shorter domain, with the plate ending at the outlet, $C_f$ on the medium grid was $+3.65\,\%$ from the value in the domain used here; two other extents changed it by $+0.30\,\%$ and $-0.16\,\%$.
The domain used here was chosen after seeing the short-domain result; two further enlargements, tried in exploratory runs not used here, did not meet the residual target on the medium grid, so the domain is not shown converged.
(iv)~The leading-edge node, which belongs to both the symmetry plane and the wall, is a stagnation node with a pressure spike whose coefficient grows with refinement (0.022, 0.032, 0.047 on the three grids) and does not converge; no single-boundary remedy is available because a no-slip wall and a symmetry plane cannot be one boundary.
(v)~For each quantity the observed order is the same within 0.12 at the stations $x=0.25$ to 0.75 (0.75 to 1.16 over all quantities), below the formal second order, so it cannot separate the order of the scheme from a leading-edge error that the boundary layer carries downstream; $C_f\sqrt{Re_x}$ on the finest grid is 0.6591, 0.6641, 0.6663 and 0.6680 at $x=0.25$, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 against Blasius 0.664, rising through the exact value with $x$, and what sets that slope was not separated.

The laminar plate therefore does not give a clean verification of the discretisation: on the finest grid the differences from Blasius are $+0.008\,\%$ on $C_f$ (at a sign crossing) and 0.4 to 1.9\,\% on the profile integrals, attributable in part, but not fully quantified, to the effects (i)--(v).

\subsection{Conservation of mass}
\label{sec:massflux}

% source: results/massflux.csv
SU2 reports the mass flux through each boundary marker.
On the planar cases (wedge, expansion) the inflow and outflow agree to the print floor of the history file (relative imbalance at or below $10^{-9}$) on every grid.
That is what a conservative scheme gives at a converged state: it tests convergence and flux bookkeeping, not the discretisation error.
On the axisymmetric cone the flux is for the full revolution and carries the $2\pi r$ weight; the imbalance is nonzero on every grid, $6.6\times10^{-4}$, $1.8\times10^{-4}$ and $4.3\times10^{-5}$ on the three grids (successive ratios 3.75 and 4.07), and $3.4\times10^{-4}$, $1.0\times10^{-4}$ and $2.6\times10^{-5}$ in the symmetry-axis variant (ratios 3.33 and 3.99).
Every value is below the tutorial's $10^{-3}$ for a consistent solution.
A ratio near four per refinement is consistent with a second-order discretisation error; with three grids it is an observation and not an observed order.
That the $2\pi r$ weight is the cause is an interpretation, not a proof; the symmetry-axis variant has 0.52 to 0.59 times the imbalance of the first, which is not explained.

\paragraph{Pipeline error.}
The layer~0 and layer~1 errors against the same exact solution are meant to be subtracted to give the \emph{pipeline error, including three-dimensional unstructured meshing}.
That cannot be done here.
Layer~1's finest level is not an accepted solution, so its error is not that of a converged solution and no subtraction is quoted; there is also no layer~1 uncertainty with which to judge whether the two layers differ.
Even with an accepted fine level the two layers differ in more than the chain: in mesh type (structured axisymmetric against unstructured tetrahedra), in domain, in the cylinder and tail, in the freestream state, in the way the wall $\Cp$ is extracted, and in the placement of the window relative to the tip in cell units.
The layer~0 GCI for the same window, 0.059\,\%, does not bound its own finest-grid error (0.188\,\%; Table~\ref{tab:l0}), so it would understate layer~0's uncertainty.
The shock angle and the axial force of the cone alone were not compared in layer~1.

\paragraph{Cut surface output.}
Observed behaviour of the product, with no cause established beyond that the write is cut when the runner stops SU2: the final surface output can be incomplete.
In the medium level the Velocity field of the surface file held 816 whole values of 15\,468 (a first medium run had its pressure-coefficient field cut and could not be read; the level used here is a second run of the same case).
For the fine level the surface file in CSV form held 1\,255 of 28\,265 complete rows, and the VTK file used is the periodic snapshot of iteration 200 while the history has 210 iterations, so there is no complete pressure source at the final iteration.
On 213 cone-wall nodes held by both files, the $\Cp$ of the two states differs by $6\times10^{-5}$ relative to the mean $\Cp$; this does not alter any gate.
The pressure-coefficient fields used were complete in every level.

\section{Application: the six steps on the sample rocket and the Basic Finner (layer 2)}
\label{sec:application}

\subsection{Step 1: examination of the code}
\label{sec:step1}

% source: no results CSV; the product's test documentation and the tests added by this study
No new calculation is made for this step.
The product's existing tests include order tests of its time integrators but do not measure the order of mesh convergence, and no test of grid-convergence code existed before this study; the CFD solver itself is not run by the tests that run in continuous integration.
This study adds tests for the GCI procedure (Section~\ref{sec:gci}), for the exact solutions against independent tables (Section~\ref{sec:cone10}), for the identity of the layer~0 numerical settings with the product's, and for the consistency checks below.

\subsection{Step 2: iterative convergence}
\label{sec:iter}

The tutorial asks that the residual falls by several orders, that the coefficients become flat, and that what remains after the iteration is stopped is small against the grid uncertainty; if the iterative error is of the order of the GCI, the grid convergence argument fails.
% source: results/layer0.csv, results/layer0_plate.csv (residual_drop_orders)
In layer~0 every run reached its residual target, with 12.7 to 13.9 orders of drop for the Euler cases and 9.4 to 10.4 for the three plate grids (Sections~\ref{sec:l0results} and~\ref{sec:plate}).
The layer~1 levels dropped the density residual by 5.52, 4.80 and 2.44 orders (the last not accepted; Section~\ref{sec:layer1}).
% source: results/iterative.csv (header: judgement; rows)
The third requirement is examined on the 38 rows of the iterative-error comparison, where the iterative error is the difference between the value at the stop and a later or deeper state of the same run, and the ratio is that error over the $\mathrm{GCI}_\fine$ of the same quantity.
\emph{No row in which the grid and the quantity match has a ratio above 1}; no GCI is invalidated by its iterative error.
Five rows with a usable GCI have ratios below 0.003; they are the Mach 2 normal-force slope of the sample rocket at the three levels and at a separate history, and one flat-plate row that is indicative only.
Only one condition therefore has a usable GCI, the Mach 2 normal-force slope, and its iterative error is a lower bound, because the reference state is the last row the solver wrote: on the finest grid it is $3.3\times10^{-4}\,\%$, $3.2\times10^{-5}$ of the GCI (10.6\,\%), and the density residual had fallen by 8.6 orders.
That is no evidence that the iterative error disturbs this GCI, but weaker than a demonstration that it is small.
For 28 rows there is no ratio because the matching GCI is refused, oscillatory, flagged or unusable: the iterative error is known there and cannot be compared.
On the sample-rocket levels it is at most 0.04\,\% on $C_A$ and 0.02\,\% on $C_N$; these are lower bounds, because the reference state is the last row the solver wrote and not a converged state.
A stop at a density residual of $10^{-6}$ from a warm start leaves a larger error: 0.55 to 1.42\,\% in $C_N$ at Mach 0.8 and $4^\circ$ (0.003 to 0.012\,\% at the deeper stops), and 6.3\,\% in $C_A$ at Mach 0.8, where no usable GCI exists for comparison.
Five flat-plate rows exceed 1 (3.11, 16.6 and 50.4 at Mach 0.2; 1.03 and 8.34 at Mach 2), but they are indicative only: they set the iterative error of the plate drag (0.12 to 1.9\,\% at Mach 0.2) against the grid uncertainty of the skin friction (0.038\,\%), which is another quantity, and no GCI of the plate drag exists.
They do not invalidate the skin-friction GCI; they show that the stop rule of that study leaves an iterative error in the drag larger than the skin-friction grid uncertainty.
Layer~0 has no iterative error in per cent, only the residual drops above.

\subsection{Step 3: consistency}
\label{sec:consistency}

% source: results/consistency.csv
\paragraph{$C_N(\alpha=0)=0$ and antisymmetry $C_N(-\alpha)=-C_N(\alpha)$.}
For a symmetric body both relations follow from the symmetry of the shape; they were checked on five configurations of an existing Euler asymmetry study at Mach 0.3, 0.8 and 2.0.
On the four configurations without mirror symmetry of the mesh (12 rows), $|C_N(0)|$ is 0.013 to 0.091, against tolerances of 0.007 to 0.012 (1\,\% of the mirror-combined normal force), and the mirror-combined even part $[C_N(+4^\circ)+C_N(-4^\circ)]/2$ is 0.014 to 0.085 in magnitude; every one of these 24 rows fails.
On the fifth configuration, a mesh made mirror-symmetric by construction, the checks pass, but they pass by construction and say nothing about the solver.
The production runs of the sample rocket use such a symmetric mesh; their $C_N(0)$ is zero to solver round-off ($|C_N|<2\times10^{-5}$) for the same reason.
The tests cannot separate a solver effect from a mesh-asymmetry effect on the asymmetric meshes.
% source: results/consistency.csv (cn_antisymmetry rows of the medium level without the mirror), results/sample_euler_levels.csv
On the sample rocket the mirror was removed from the medium mesh and both $+4^\circ$ and $-4^\circ$ were solved, so that antisymmetry is a test of the solver.
The even part is $-0.0388$ at Mach 0.3 and $-0.0201$ at Mach 0.8, against tolerances of 0.0114 and 0.0118: both fail.
At Mach 2 it is $+0.0015$ against 0.0093 and passes.
The even part is of the size of the same mesh's $C_N(0)$ ($-0.0404$, $-0.0219$ and $+0.0016$), which is consistent with an offset that the mesh asymmetry carries at every incidence; which part of it is the mesh and which the solver was not separated.
Some solves have the product's status ``accepted'' (an early stop with three orders of residual drop and flat coefficients) and enter as written: the $-4^\circ$ solve at Mach 0.3, the zero-incidence solve at Mach 0.8, and both at Mach 2.

\paragraph{d'Alembert's paradox.}
% source: results/consistency.csv (ca_dalembert row)
At Mach 0.3 and zero incidence the inviscid axial force coefficient is expected to be zero (d'Alembert's paradox).
% source: results/consistency.csv (ca_dalembert_refined rows), results/gci_sample_euler.csv
A shrinking $|C_A|$ would point to a discretisation error, and a value that does not shrink to a defect of formulation or boundary conditions.
On the three levels of the sample-rocket family $C_A=-0.0628$, $-0.0455$ and $-0.0305$ (coarse to fine): $|C_A|$ shrinks, by factors of 1.38 and 1.49.
Its extrapolation, however, crosses the exact value: $+0.045$ on one grid-size definition and $+0.065$ on the other, with GCI of 309 and 393\,\%, so the row is marked unusable.
Three grids that are not similar and an extrapolation past zero support the trend, not a convergence to zero.
At Mach 0.8, where no exact value is assumed, $C_A=-0.0326$, $-0.0226$, $-0.0128$ and the GCI (over 1\,600\,\%) is unusable.
The mass-flux balance was checked on the layer~0 cases (Section~\ref{sec:massflux}); it was not checked on the layer~2 far field.

\subsection{Step 4: spatial convergence}
\label{sec:grid}

\subsubsection{Reynolds-averaged sample-rocket family from existing data}

% source: results/gci_existing.csv (rans_mesh_study rows)
An existing three-level RANS family of the sample rocket (wall size 8, 4.8 and 3\,mm; Mach 0.8 and 2; incidence $0^\circ$ and $\pm4^\circ$, the $4^\circ$ values combined from $+4^\circ$ and $-4^\circ$ solutions) was passed through the convergence gate.
% source: results/gci_existing.csv (rans_mesh_study rows, note column)
All four families are refused. At Mach 0.8, $C_N$, $C_A$ and $x_{cp}$ at $4^\circ$ are refused because the 8\,mm level's $-4^\circ$ solve was recorded as not converged; at Mach 0.8 and 2, $C_N(0)$ is refused because the $0^\circ$ solve was recorded as not converged at 8\,mm and 4.8\,mm; at Mach 2, $C_N$, $C_A$ and $x_{cp}$ at $4^\circ$ are refused because the 3\,mm level's $-4^\circ$ solve is a rerun with a fixed iteration count and no recorded verdict or residual drop.
The data file records the solver's verdict for each solve but no residual drop.

The product's convergence verdict ``unconverged'' for a RANS case covers two kinds of solve, which its documentation separates: a \emph{stall}, in which the residual stops short of the required drop while the coefficients are flat, and a \emph{limit cycle}, in which the residual has dropped far enough and the coefficients oscillate in a small band; for either, the window average is used and a warning is issued.
A stall therefore fails the tutorial's residual-drop condition and a limit cycle its coefficient-flatness condition, and the record does not say which kind the solve was.
% source: the product's CFD documentation, section on the Reynolds-averaged acceptance verdict (not a results CSV)
The consequence is stated narrowly.
The grid-convergence index published for this family at Mach 0.8 rests on a coarse-level value that combines a $-4^\circ$ solve recorded as not converged; whichever kind of solve it was, that GCI does not meet the tutorial's iterative-convergence precondition (a converged solution), and the documentation does not say so.
The study's own order rule would also flag the published Mach 0.8 $C_N$ triplet, whose observed order is 4.33, above 4.
% source: the product's CFD documentation and doc/data/cfd/rans_mesh_study.json (not a results CSV)
This is evidence that the precondition is not demonstrated; it is not a claim that the published number is wrong.

\subsection{Step 5: temporal convergence}
\label{sec:temporal}

% source: results/temporal.csv
The CFD is steady, so temporal convergence in the tutorial's sense does not apply to it; there is no time step in the SU2 runs.
The product's time-dependent part is the six-degree-of-freedom flight integrator, and its convergence is examined by step-size refinement.
The sample rocket is flown with a fourth-order Runge--Kutta integrator with steps of 8, 4, 2 and 1\,ms ($r=2$; parachute descent, 4\,m/s wind), giving two triplets, 1/2/4\,ms and 2/4/8\,ms.
The quantities are apogee, apogee time, landing distance, flight time, rail-exit time and speed, and altitude and speed at fixed times.
No order was obtained for the sample rocket: every row is refused, as the differences change sign (oscillatory) or two steps give identical output at its printed resolution.
Over the four step sizes the apogee altitude varies by $0.018$\,m in $4440.5$\,m ($4.1\times10^{-6}$ relative), the landing distance by 0.032\,m in 7735.5\,m and the flight time by 2.4\,ms in 685\,s.
That is a spread, not an error estimate: without a convergence order no band is claimed.

The scatter is attributed, on the evidence of controls, to the rail model: the rocket is held at rest on the rail while the thrust is below its weight, which puts a kink in the acceleration at about 2.3\,ms that the integrator does not locate and that falls in a different step relative to the step ends in each run.
Three controls modify the motor thrust curve (not the product's sample): removing both the hold and the 0 to 0.05\,s thrust ramp gives orders of 4.01 for apogee and 3.96 for flight time on the coarse triplet, as expected of a fourth-order scheme; removing only the hold reduces the rail-exit speed difference between the 8 and 4\,ms runs from $4.5\times10^{-4}$ to $4.1\times10^{-8}$\,m/s, which supports the hold as the main cause of the rail-exit scatter; and a fully nested sweep rules out the thrust-curve knots that the step does not divide as the dominant cause.
A second, smaller contribution is not excluded: with only the hold removed, the coarse-triplet apogee is still non-monotone.
The finest triplets of the controls stop converging at about $10^{-9}$ to $10^{-8}$ relative, a floor that persists on the nested sweep and whose cause is unidentified; the one order from a finest triplet (4.75 for the apogee time of the first control) has differences below that floor and the same run's apogee oscillates, so it is not evidence of fourth-order convergence.
This is verification of the integrator by step refinement, not a comparison with measured data.

\section{Validation and model uncertainty}
\label{sec:modelvalid}

\subsection{Step 6: model uncertainty}
\label{sec:model}

The product offers Euler and RANS with the Spalart--Allmaras model, and nothing else.
The tutorial's request for a comparison of several turbulence models is therefore answered only in part, and the limitation is a limit of the product's scope, not an omission.
% source: results/turbulence_models.csv (header and rows)
Because the product cannot run another model, Spalart--Allmaras is compared with the Menter SST model on the turbulent flat plate with SU2 run directly (Mach 0.2, $Re=5\times10^{6}$ per metre, adiabatic wall, the zero-pressure-gradient plate of the NASA Turbulence Modeling Resource \citep{tmr}, station $x=0.97$).
The numerical settings are those of the product's start-up and second-order stages, copied from product-written configurations; the two models differ only in the model and the keys that SST needs.
The SST variant is V1994m, that of SU2's own test case for this plate; SU2's default variant was not run.
The grids are not those of the Turbulence Modeling Resource: they have its extent and its node counts ($69\times49$, $137\times97$, $273\times193$) and boundary conditions, are exactly nested ($r=2$), and have a point distribution chosen here (maximum $y^+$ 1.32, 0.72, 0.41).
Both models stop at the same rule, a density residual 8 orders below the start-up peak, not the product's acceptance rule; the residual drop of the weakest equation is 7.5 to 8.0 orders for Spalart--Allmaras and 5.5 to 5.6 for SST.
The skin friction at $x=0.97$ differs by $-3.70$, $-1.72$ and $-0.79\,\%$ (SST against Spalart--Allmaras) on the coarse, medium and fine grid.
On the fine grid the SST GCI is 1.14\,\%, so a difference of this size is not separable from SST's own numerical uncertainty; SST is first order here (observed order 1.02), and its last refinement moved the skin friction by 0.94\,\%, comparable with the difference.
The Spalart--Allmaras GCI is flagged (observed order 5.3, beyond twice the formal order; GCI $8\times10^{-5}\,\%$), so its band is not used and no verdict is written.
The plate drag differs by $-0.46\,\%$ on the fine grid against GCI bands of 0.67\,\% (Spalart--Allmaras) and 0.48\,\% (SST); the difference does not exceed their sum and cannot be separated from the grid uncertainty.
The extrapolated values differ by $+0.11\,\%$ in skin friction and $+0.46\,\%$ in drag; they are estimates and no verdict is written.
The comparison is between two models and is not validation.
Against the published Turbulence Modeling Resource value for Spalart--Allmaras at this station (the mean of two other solvers on a $545\times385$ grid), the fine-grid skin friction here is $+0.80\,\%$ higher, a cross-comparison in which the grid, the numerics and the model variant differ: the product's model is the negative-viscosity form with the $f_{t2}$ term, not the plain model of the reference.
The same runs agree with the existing Spalart--Allmaras data on the Turbulence Modeling Resource grids to 0.006\,\% in skin friction on the fine grid (other grids, other numerics).

Comparing Euler with RANS-SA on the same condition is a cross-comparison of two models; a difference between them counts as a model difference only if it exceeds the sum of the two numerical uncertainty bands.
% source: results/model_uncertainty.csv (rows and header)
Seven Euler-against-RANS conditions were set against the two numerical bands: the sample rocket at Mach 0.8 and 2 at $4^\circ$ ($C_N$ and $x_{cp}$) and the Basic Finner at Mach 1.832 ($C_N$ and $x_{cp}$ at $2^\circ$, the axial force at $0^\circ$).
The differences (RANS against Euler) are $+4.99$ and $+0.27\,\%$ for the sample rocket at Mach 0.8, $+3.30$ and $+0.017\,\%$ at Mach 2, and for the Basic Finner $+0.22\,\%$ in $C_N$, $-0.73\,\%$ in $x_{cp}$ and $+54\,\%$ in the axial coefficient.
\emph{None of the seven has a verdict.}
The RANS band does not exist for the sample rocket, because every triplet of that study is refused, and not for the Basic Finner, because no RANS GCI was obtained at Mach 1.832.
The Euler band is missing or flagged for the sample rocket (not converging, oscillatory, or an order outside 0.5 to 4) and absent for the Basic Finner at Mach 1.832, because its only Euler grid study is at Mach 2.375.
The sample-rocket comparison also sets a RANS value on a 3\,mm mesh against an Euler value on a 2\,mm mesh; the Basic Finner axial coefficient mixes the flow model with the modelled base term of the Euler mode.
The sizes of the differences are therefore reported, and whether they are a model difference or numerical error is not decided.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the GCI can be trusted for.}
% source: results/layer0_gci.csv (counts, true_error_pct, gci_bounds_error; the ratio of 8 is derived from gci_fine_pct and true_error_pct of the expansion wall pressure)
The central result of layer~0 is a measurement of the GCI itself: where the exact answer is known it bounded the true error in 5 of 7 inviscid cases and 2 of 5 laminar-plate cases.
The two inviscid failures were not both flagged by the order rule or by the window-stability test (Table~\ref{tab:detect}), and holding is not tightness: the bounds that held were up to eight times the error.
A GCI computed in layer~2, without an exact answer, is therefore a conditional estimate that can understate the true error, and should be read with the flags of Section~\ref{sec:gci} and not as a certificate.

\paragraph{Wall quantities and junctions.}
% source: results/layer0.csv (expansion wall_mach, wall_p_ratio and diag_entropy_wall_window rows); results/layer0_gci.csv (cone and cone_sym_axis wall_cp true_error_pct: 0.188 and 0.748, a factor of about four)
A wall entropy excess that does not change with the grid accompanies a wall Mach error of 3.55\,\% on the expansion that does not decrease with refinement and for which no GCI exists, while the surface pressure, from which forces are integrated, has an error of 0.01\,\% there; the interpretation (entropy generated at a corner or tip and convected along the wall) is consistent with the data, not proven.
The tip-junction sensitivity of the cone (a factor of four in the finest-grid wall pressure error) was found in a two-dimensional structured setup.
It shows that a junction of two boundary types can dominate an error that a grid study would attribute to the resolution; it does not show that the product's three-dimensional meshes are affected.

\paragraph{Grids the product can build.}
% source: results/gci_existing.csv (header and basic_finner_mesh_check rows)
Because the product fixes the largest cell size, refinement through its settings changes only the near-wall region; the layer~1 cone-cylinder levels grow by 1.57 and 1.67 in nodes against 2.74 for a similar refinement.
Every GCI of such a family depends on an arbitrary choice of the grid-size definition, and on the Basic Finner the orders of the two triplets disagree under both.
A reliable GCI of the product would need a mesh control that scales the whole field together with the wall size; that is a change to the product and outside the scope of this study.

\paragraph{Reference data.}
% source: results/cone10_reference_check.csv; Celik et al. Table 1 cell: tests/test_gci.py (not a results CSV)
Two published verification references each contain an internally inconsistent entry: the NPARC 10$^\circ$ cone table (Section~\ref{sec:cone10}) and one cell of Table~1 of \citet{celik2008}.
Reporting them is not a criticism of either; it matters for what can be used as a target, and it is why the reference values here are computed and checked against independent tables.

\paragraph{Findings about the product.}
% source: the product's CFD documentation and doc/data/cfd/rans_mesh_study.json; results/layer1_cone.csv; results/basic_finner_rans_levels.csv (not a results CSV where stated in the sections cited)
The grid-convergence index published for the RANS sample-rocket family at Mach 0.8 rests on a solve recorded as not converged and so does not meet the tutorial's iterative-convergence precondition; this is not a claim that the published number is wrong (Section~\ref{sec:grid}).
When the runner stops SU2, the final surface output can be incomplete; no cause is established (Section~\ref{sec:layer1}).
With RANS and a fin size smaller than the wall size, the mesher aborts, and whether the product's input or gmsh is at fault was not separated (Section~\ref{sec:l12setup}).
The Taylor--Maccoll solver of the documentation brackets the shock angle by a limit that lies on the wrong side near detachment; the 10$^\circ$ cones here are far from that range (Section~\ref{sec:exact}).
The existing validation figure of the documentation draws a bar of length zero for a quantity whose error is unknown (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).

\paragraph{Limits of this study.}
Layer~0 verifies SU2 with the product's settings and not the product's pipeline.
The laminar plate verifies a configuration the product does not write, and the turbulent-plate data come from a configuration close to but not identical to the product's.
The 2-D boundary-junction and entropy findings are untested on the three-dimensional meshes.
The product cannot run a second turbulence model; the comparison of Spalart--Allmaras with SST was made on a flat plate with self-built grids and with SU2 directly, not through the product, SST is first order there and one of the two bands is flagged.
The pipeline error was not assessed, because the finest layer~1 level did not converge.
The validation rests on a single measured data set (DREV-TM-9703), and for $\CXzero$ the CFD has no band, so whether it overlaps the measurement cannot be judged.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The conclusions supported at this stage are the following.
% source: results/layer0.csv, results/layer0_gci.csv, results/cone10_reference_check.csv, results/cone_shock_angle_sources.csv, results/gci_existing.csv, results/temporal.csv (every number in the list); figures/validation_bands.svg (item 6)
\begin{enumerate}
\item With the product's numerical settings, SU2 reproduces exact inviscid solutions to within 0.2\,\% on the surface pressure and the shock angle at 257$\times$193 nodes in the primary setups (0.75\,\% on the cone with a symmetry-axis tip junction), while wall quantities derived from the Mach number retain errors of 0.8 to 3.6\,\% that decrease slowly or not at all.
\item A GCI bounded the true finest-grid error for 5 of 7 inviscid results and for 2 of 5 laminar-plate results, the latter only at sign crossings (and the five plate rows are not independent); neither the observed order nor the stability of the order across windows detects both failures.
\item The NPARC 10$^\circ$ cone table is internally inconsistent: its shock angle disagrees with independent tables, which support 26.74$^\circ$ and not 27.18$^\circ$, and its pressure, temperature and density ratios disagree with the Taylor--Maccoll solution computed here; only its surface Mach number agrees.
\item Grids built through the product's settings cannot be refined similarly; the four-level Basic Finner Euler study does not establish a reliable GCI, and the published RANS sample-rocket grid study fails the convergence gate for every condition for which it was tested.
\item The flight integrator shows no convergence order on the sample rocket, attributed on the evidence of controls mainly to the rail model, with a second contribution not excluded.
\item The Euler Basic Finner $\CNa$ lies 2.9 to 10.5\,\% below the free-flight measurement and its axial-force coefficient 15 to 28\,\% below it; the $\CNa$ difference exceeds the tabulated probable error (a fit statistic, conventionally a 50\,\% bound, that excludes round-to-round scatter and systematic error) at all eight Mach numbers, but cannot be attributed to model form rather than numerical error, because no established numerical band exists.
\item Through the product's chain, the 10$^\circ$ cone-cylinder at Mach 2.35 did not converge on its finest level, so layer~1 has no GCI and the pipeline error, including three-dimensional unstructured meshing, is not quantified; the accepted levels differ from the exact cone $\Cp$ by $-2.51$ and $-1.26\,\%$ (descriptive).
\item On the sample rocket the Euler grids cannot be refined similarly (cell growth 1.74 and 1.77 against 2.74), and the only GCI that is neither refused nor unusable is the Mach 2 normal-force slope, 10.6 to 16.3\,\% depending on the grid-size definition, the larger flagged as outside the asymptotic range (the Mach 2 centre-of-pressure GCI is flagged and not used); $|C_A|$ at Mach 0.3 shrinks under refinement but its extrapolation crosses zero.
\item No GCI was obtained for the Basic Finner with RANS at Mach 1.832, so the validation comparison has no RANS band and the overlap of numerical and measured uncertainty cannot be judged.
\item The iterative error does not invalidate any GCI that has a matching iterative-error record (no matched ratio above 1), but only one condition has a usable GCI and its iterative error is a lower bound; five flat-plate comparisons above 1 pair the drag stopping error with the skin-friction GCI and are indicative only.
\item None of the seven Euler-against-RANS comparisons can be given a verdict, because one or both numerical bands are missing or flagged; on the flat plate, SST differs from Spalart--Allmaras by $-0.79\,\%$ in skin friction (within the SST GCI of 1.14\,\%, with the Spalart--Allmaras GCI flagged) and $-0.46\,\%$ in drag (not separable from the grid uncertainty).
\end{enumerate}
